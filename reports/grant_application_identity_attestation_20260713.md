## Short summary

I am building an identity-attestation layer that certifies whether a monitor, reward feature, or mechanistic edit coordinate still refers to what it meant when registered. Cryptographic hashes certify bytes and behavioral monitors certify selected statistics; neither establishes semantic continuity across registries, contexts, or model checkpoints.

The control harness, mathematical bound, certificate API, tamper generators, edit-sectioning tool, and preregistered VPD design now exist. Every current result is synthetic. Funding would support the transition from validated instruments to carefully bounded real-model tests while removing the incentive to depend financially on capability-positive compactification or self-improvement results.

## Problem

A signed evidence item can retain the same payload and valid signature while a registry change alters what it authorizes. A learned feature can preserve its spectrum and pairwise transfer quality while its signed coordinate rotates around a context-by-checkpoint loop. An attribution-guided edit can therefore target a coordinate whose identity is unstable even when conventional validation remains green.

This creates a gap between cryptographic integrity and behavioral monitoring. The proposed layer records a reference basis, selected spectral band, transport lineage, loop holonomy, orientation component, uncertainty, and audit coverage. Signed interventions require holonomy-clean certification; gauge-invariant energy rewards require lineage certification; weaker evidence is labelled engineering evidence.

## Verified work

### Hash-preserving re-anchoring control

A sealed deterministic administrative-control benchmark contains 2,400 episodes and 120 armed semantic re-anchoring cases. The planted attacks preserve evidence payload hashes and signatures while changing current denotation.

- Plain evidence gate: 100% attack success on armed planted cases.
- Identity-attested gate: 0% attack success.
- Aligned accuracy: 100% for both gates.
- Escalation cost: 17.5% for attestation versus 12.5% for the plain gate.

This is synthetic positive-control evidence, not evidence of endogenous model misalignment.

### Mathematical control bound

For edge worst-direction retentions `W_e`, realized signed transport `M`, and loop holonomy `H`, the project proves

`||M-I|| <= sum_e(1-sqrt(W_e)) + ||H-I||`.

For orientation-preserving holonomy, `||H-I|| = 2 sin(alpha_max/2)`; orientation reversal reaches maximal displacement. With simultaneously valid measurement bounds, authorizing below this conservative bound provides a false-authorization guarantee.

A preregistered 6,000-fixture CPU red team produced 792 authorizations, all inside the control-loss budget, with zero false authorizations, 13.2% coverage, and zero unsafe authorizations over a 2,800-point boundary grid. The complete vector closed planted lineage-only and holonomy-only blind spots.

### Holonomy-bounded edit sectioning

The sectioning tool replaces one assumed global signed coordinate with one transported coordinate per measured flat patch. Version 0.3 analytically calibrates the pooling bias-variance crossover.

- The recoupled fixture reproduced the patch advantage over global and independent edits.
- Four deliberately decoupled fixtures made the patch plan lose at analytically derived low-variance gate points.
- The complete 180-cell empirical surface matched the analytic sign away from zero.
- An unmeasured connector between two patches received the required boundary-impact score and top audit priority.

This establishes that the instrument does not manufacture a universal utility advantage. It remains synthetic and does not show successful transformer editing.

## Three falsifiable bets

1. Identity attestation will detect broader model-mediated re-anchoring attacks without unacceptable aligned-usefulness loss.
2. Holonomy sectioning will improve edit utility only where its measured variance advantage exceeds pooling bias.
3. Attribution-guided VPD edits will outperform matched random controls only, or more strongly, inside identity-clean strata.

The bets are reported independently. Failure will not be pooled, redefined, or hidden behind the validated instruments.

## Risks and claim boundaries

- Identity geometry may not predict causal persistence on real models. That is a publishable null under the registered protocol.
- Synthetic fixtures may overstate utility. The decoupled sectioning fixtures explicitly test and bound that failure.
- Conservative gates may authorize too little. Precision and coverage are always reported together, and audit placement targets uncertainty rather than silently weakening thresholds.
- A real-model construction may fail or exceed resources. Construction, geometry, and cost failures are distinct outcomes.
- Identity measurements cannot prove general self-improvement risk. Downstream behavior requires grouped held-out outcomes and nuisance baselines.
- Capability-positive compactification findings may be dual-use. Such results will be handled through responsible disclosure rather than treated as publication incentives.

## Why funding is useful now

The architecture is no longer speculative, but the real-model premise remains untested. Funding buys protected time to resolve registered bets, pay for independent scrutiny, publish negative findings, and avoid making financial survival depend on producing a capability-positive result.

The grant will deliver either evidence that identity attestation improves real control decisions or a well-instrumented negative result identifying where spectral identity methods stop working. Both outcomes are useful to AI-control research.
