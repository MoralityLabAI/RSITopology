# ASMP-9 resolution-obligation matrix after v0.47

## Verdict

**ASMP-9 remains unresolved.**

Version v0.47 closes the v0.46 request for a matching decision-deficiency
modulus at one finite, registered sample atom. Uniform confidence coverage
forces an exact coupled parameter set; propagating that set through the full
decision risk gives a lower bound, and both a pointwise confidence set and the
minimum endpoint of a nondecreasing Buehler bound attain it. The result also
shows that concentration slack can reverse a sample-allocation comparison.

The theorem is conditional on a finite grid, deterministic confidence
procedures, one total-error ordering, iid known binary channels, and the
all-zero atom. It does not yet provide the uniform access characterization
required by ASMP-9.

## What v0.47 establishes

For any deterministic confidence set with coverage at least `1-alpha`, the
all-zero observation forces inclusion of every finite-grid channel satisfying

```text
product_q (1-p_q)^n_q > alpha.
```

The matching spike construction proves pointwise sharpness. Under total
observed errors as a frozen ordered statistic,

```text
U(t)=max {d(p):P_p(T<=t)>alpha}
```

is the smallest nondecreasing uniformly honest direct upper bound. Its
minimum endpoint is the same sharp modulus.

At the disjoint `N=72` confirmation, the exact four-class modulus is `0.13`
under `(30,21,21)` and approximately `0.112709` under uniform `(24,24,24)`.
The inherited method-of-types upper ranks those allocations in the opposite
order. For root-group loss, decision-directed allocation `(70,1,1)` lowers
the exact modulus from `0.10` to `0.04`.

Thus v0.47 supplies the matching finite lower construction absent from v0.46
and proves that the earlier upper-bound slack is allocation dependent.

## What v0.47 does not establish

- Optimality of total errors among possible evidence orderings.
- A globally optimal `N=72` sharp-modulus allocation.
- A confidence modulus at nonzero observed counts.
- A continuous-channel or asymptotic minimax theorem.
- Improvement or non-improvement by randomized confidence procedures under
  another criterion.
- Robustness to correlated, adaptive, or strategic channel misspecification.
- Existence of a calibrated real preference channel.

## Fixed resolution-directed sequence

1. **Completed through v0.47:** finite decision-relative object; static and
   adaptive known-channel characterization; finite-sample certification;
   sentinel rate; generator-specific information; decision-directed design;
   exact coupled image; and a matching sharp modulus at the all-zero atom.
2. Extend the modulus from one atom/statistic to the full registered sample
   experiment, or prove a statistic-independent lower/upper comparison.
3. Add a frozen correlated/adaptive/strategic misspecification neighborhood
   and prove robust transfer or a matching impossibility.
4. Extend the scalar-versus-relation-versus-no-object trichotomy.
5. Re-enter real-model measurement only through a prospectively registered,
   calibrated channel.

## Next load-bearing target

Version v0.48 should test whether the atom result is exceptional or the
endpoint of a uniform finite theorem. Freeze a small enough channel grid and
sample budget to compute the complete distribution of total errors and:

1. construct the full Buehler table at every statistic value;
2. compare it with lower bounds that do not assume the registered ordering;
3. include a counter-ordering or randomized-procedure control;
4. determine whether one ordering is uniformly decision-optimal or whether
   ordering choice is itself decision dependent; and
5. separate exact confidence coverage from computationally efficient
   evaluation.

If the registered ordering is not uniformly optimal, the required outcome is
an exact witness and a replacement access object—not another convenient
radius.

## Claim boundary

Version v0.47 is classical finite confidence theory specialized to a finite
ASMP-9 decision experiment. It closes a matching conditional modulus target,
not general value identifiability, strategic behavioral misspecification,
real preference access, or ASMP-9.
