# ASMP-9 resolution audit after v0.8

Date: 2026-07-28

## Verdict

**ASMP-9 is not resolved.**

Version v0.8 closes a narrow population-level response-link boundary. A known
injective link with unknown positive inverse temperature identifies utility
up to translation and positive scale. An unrestricted unknown monotone link
on a finite design admits exact observational equivalences between
non-affinely-related utilities. This strengthens the program's no-go side but
does not supply the broad two-sided classification required by ASMP-9.

## Canonical resolution obligations

The non-normative v0.2 candidate statement requires:

1. the maximal invariance group for each registered data source;
2. necessary and sufficient query/environment interventions for quotient
   identification;
3. sharp query, sample, and intervention-order bounds;
4. robustness to a frozen class of behavioral misspecification; and
5. a no-go theorem when the demonstrator class admits no coherent latent value
   object of the declared kind.

## Requirement-by-requirement audit

### 1. Maximal invariance groups

**Partially established.**

Ordinary and discounted potential shaping, additive constants, positive scale,
and the finite-design unknown-link equivalence are characterized for their
registered sources. Version v0.8 also proves that positive affine reward gauge
is not maximal when the monotone response link itself is unrestricted.

Still missing are maximal invariances for general deterministic and stochastic
policies, trajectory demonstrations, mixed sources, entropy regularization,
transition redistribution, and contextual or history-sensitive response
models.

### 2. Necessary and sufficient access

**Partially established.**

The suite has sharp access theorems for direct loop returns, bounded ordinal
comparisons, one known noisy comparison channel, discounted
boundary-signature functionals, exact pairwise log-odds, and a scalar
threshold-policy oracle. Version v0.8 gives an impossibility result for every
finite complete pairwise design under an unrestricted monotone link.

It does not characterize the weakest link normalization or intervention family
that restores identification. General finite-MDP policy and demonstration
access, multiple transition kernels and discount factors, and joint data
sources remain open.

### 3. Sharp query, sample, and intervention-order bounds

**Partially established.**

Coefficient width is sharp in v0.3.1 and v0.4. Version v0.5.1 gives
finite-sample upper and lower bounds and an adaptive/nonadaptive separation.
Version v0.7 gives sharp deterministic-threshold ambiguity rates. Version v0.8
shows that no amount of repeated sampling repairs an exactly identical
population law, but it is not itself a finite-sample rate theorem.

Missing are tight behavioral estimation constants, unknown-link or
unknown-temperature confidence regions, dependent-response bounds, and
general-MDP intervention-order bounds.

### 4. Behavioral misspecification robustness

**Not established at resolution scope.**

Version v0.7 exactly detects cycle inconsistency. Version v0.8 gives a
semiparametric nuisance-link obstruction: changing an unrestricted monotone
link can absorb non-affine changes in utility on a finite design. Neither
result provides a misspecification ball with a downstream reward-error
guarantee.

Finite-sample Hodge confidence regions, normalized unknown-link estimation,
contextual and history-dependent choice, correlated responses, and rules for
selecting scalar versus richer preference objects remain open.

### 5. No-go for incoherent or nonidentifiable latent values

**Established for two narrow classes; open generally.**

- Nonzero log-odds circulation proves that no scalar Bradley-Terry value
  generates the registered response field.
- Under an unrestricted unknown monotone link, two non-affine three-item
  utility rays can generate the exact same complete population law.
- Finite deterministic policy outputs cannot exactly identify an arbitrary
  continuous reward coordinate.

Still missing are no-go theorems for broader stochastic-choice,
non-expected-utility, contextual, dependent, and history-sensitive
demonstrators, together with a positive classification of when a coherent
shared scalar does exist.

## Highest-value next sequence

### v0.9: finite-sample coherence certification

Estimate edge log-odds from binomial comparisons, propagate simultaneous
intervals through a registered cycle basis, and distinguish:

- certified coherent within tolerance;
- certified circulation alternative; and
- inconclusive.

The theorem target is graph-dependent sample complexity with multiplicity
control, not failure-to-reject rhetoric.

### v0.10: general finite-MDP intervention access

Freeze a finite tabular MDP family and compare optimal-policy identities,
stochastic policy probabilities, multiple transition kernels, multiple
discount factors, and trajectory preferences. Report the exact rank or
polyhedral dimension of the remaining reward quotient and position it below
existing general identifiability theory.

### v0.11: contextual/history-sensitive no-go

Construct a declared response class in which each context separately admits a
scalar, no shared scalar exists, and a context-indexed preference kernel does.
The decisive result should include a sharp access threshold separating the
shared-scalar and context-indexed hypotheses.

## Completion criterion

ASMP-9 cannot be marked complete because each current theorem closes only one
specialized observation grammar. Resolution still requires a broad frozen
family of data sources and behavioral models, maximal source-specific
invariances, matched upper and lower access bounds, a finite-sample
misspecification theorem, and a two-sided scalar-existence/no-go
classification. Current evidence does not meet that standard.
