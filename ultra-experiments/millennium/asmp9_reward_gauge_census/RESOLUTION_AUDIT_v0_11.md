# ASMP-9 resolution audit after v0.11

Date: 2026-07-28

## Verdict

**ASMP-9 is not resolved.**

Version v0.11 closes the registered finite contextual scalar-gluing
subproblem. It gives a two-sided existence theorem: context-local scalar
representations glue to one shared scalar exactly when the independent
mixed-context cycle circulations vanish. It also gives the sharp number of
additional checks and a minimal incompatibility witness.

The result is exact finite graph linear algebra beneath established preference
aggregation and local-to-global theory. It does not establish a general value
identifiability theorem for human or model behavior.

## Effect on the five obligations

### 1. Maximal invariance groups

**Improved but partial.** The finite contextual result separates local gauge
freedom from the existence of a shared scalar and computes the quotient
dimension. Together with v0.10, it covers exact potential-shaping
intersections and finite context gluing. It does not yet unify unknown link,
temperature, shaping, context, and history transformations into one maximal
invariance classification.

### 2. Necessary and sufficient access

**Substantially improved, still partial.** The registered finite exact models
now have sharp access results for comparison graphs, noisy sign-and-tie
choices, direct returns, known soft policies across environments, and
contextual compatibility. There is no matching general theorem for passive or
adaptive sequential demonstrations with latent context.

### 3. Sharp query, sample, and intervention-order bounds

**Improved but partial.** Version v0.11 supplies the sharp exact count `q` for
mixed-context checks. Earlier versions supply sharp width and direct-query
thresholds plus a conservative finite-sample coherence certificate. Matching
minimax finite-sample bounds across the richer observation models remain open.

### 4. Behavioral misspecification robustness

**Still not established at resolution scope.** Unknown monotone links,
deterministic-policy access, finite-sample incoherence, and contextual
non-gluing each have explicit obstructions. There is no unified robustness
theorem propagating response-model error, policy-estimation error, latent
context error, and approximate rationality into a calibrated reward quotient.

### 5. No-go classification

**Improved but incomplete.** The program now has exact no-go witnesses for
unknown monotone response links, weak action-identity access, finite continuous
reward coordinates, and context-local-but-not-global scalarity. It does not
classify arbitrary history-sensitive, inconsistent, non-EU, or strategically
reported preferences.

## Highest-value remaining sequence

### v0.12: unified finite invariance lattice

Combine the finite observation classes already solved—direct comparisons,
known and unknown response links, discounted shaping, exact soft policies,
action identities, and context labels—into one typed access lattice. For every
declared access morphism:

- compute the compatible-reward quotient;
- prove monotonicity under access refinement;
- identify when two access paths have the same quotient;
- provide a witness whenever a refinement strictly reduces ambiguity; and
- distinguish scalar nonexistence from scalar nonidentifiability.

This would consolidate separate exact results into one maximal finite
invariance theorem rather than adding another isolated census.

### v0.13: approximate and sequential closure

After the exact lattice is frozen, add:

- finite-sample lower bounds matching the conservative v0.9 upper bound;
- approximate cycle and context-gluing stability;
- latent-context misspecification;
- adaptive environment and comparison allocation;
- sequential/history-dependent observation kernels; and
- explicit failure modes for inconsistent or non-EU demonstrators.

## Completion criterion

Current results cover exact comparison access, finite noisy choices,
discounted shaping, unknown response links, known finite soft policies,
deterministic-policy obstructions, and finite contextual scalar existence.
A defensible resolution still requires a unified maximal invariance
classification, matching upper and lower access bounds over a materially broad
source family, approximate and finite-sample misspecification guarantees, and
a sequential no-go/positive theorem for history-sensitive behavior.
