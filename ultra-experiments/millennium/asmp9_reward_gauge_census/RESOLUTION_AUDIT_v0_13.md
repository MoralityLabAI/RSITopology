# ASMP-9 resolution audit after v0.13

Date: 2026-07-28

## Verdict

**ASMP-9 is not resolved.**

Version v0.13 adds an exact conditional finite-sample experiment for the
Bradley-Terry comparison model. Conditioning on vertex win balance removes
the scalar-value nuisance exactly, while the realized count fiber determines
which cycle-space directions remain identifiable.

The result is classical conditional exponential-family and graph linear
algebra specialized to a prospectively registered finite access grammar. It
is not a general value-identifiability or inverse-reinforcement-learning
theorem.

## Effect on the five obligations

### 1. Maximal invariance groups

**Improved but partial.** Version v0.13 identifies the exact scalar-gradient
nuisance removed by a sufficient statistic and the remaining conditional
cycle quotient. It does not unify reward shaping, response temperature,
unknown links, context, history, and non-expected-utility transformations
into one maximal invariance classification.

### 2. Necessary and sufficient access

**Substantially improved within one finite experiment.** Fiber differences
span exactly the quotient directions visible after conditioning, yielding a
necessary and sufficient rank test for full cycle-space access. The condition
is realized-data-dependent and assumes a fixed comparison graph, fixed counts,
known item identities, and a known logistic link.

### 3. Sharp query, sample, and intervention-order bounds

**Improved but partial.** The one-cycle family admits exact finite-sample
tests and rational power calculations. The registered sample counts are
calibration points, not monotone thresholds, and no matching minimax lower
bound or optimal adaptive graph-allocation theorem is supplied.

### 4. Behavioral misspecification robustness

**Not materially closed.** Conditional nuisance removal is exact if the
Bradley-Terry likelihood is correct. It does not cover link misspecification,
dependent samples, latent contexts, strategic responses, policy-estimation
error, or non-EU demonstrators. Non-rejection remains explicitly insufficient
to certify that a scalar value exists.

### 5. No-go classification

**Improved but incomplete.** The fiber-rank theorem is an exact no-go for
treating every conditioned dataset as equally informative: singleton and
rank-deficient fibers provably cannot expose the full scalarity quotient. It
is not a no-go classification for arbitrary history-sensitive or inconsistent
behavior.

## Highest-value remaining sequence

The next load-bearing work should combine the now-separate finite-sample
pieces without weakening their status distinctions:

- integrate unconditional availability with conditional test power;
- derive matching lower bounds or optimal allocation rules for graph and
  trial selection;
- add dependent-response and overdispersion alternatives;
- treat latent or uncertain context assignments;
- connect conditional comparison access to sequential finite-MDP behavior;
  and
- prove a positive or impossibility result under a materially broader
  misspecification class.

## Completion criterion

Current results cover exact and ordinal comparison access, finite noisy
choices, discounted shaping, unknown response links, deterministic-policy
obstructions, finite contextual scalar existence, robust gluing geometry, and
conditional finite-sample quotient access.

A defensible resolution still requires a maximal invariance classification
over a materially broad behavioral source family, matching upper and lower
access bounds, finite-sample and misspecification guarantees, and a
sequential no-go/positive theorem for history-sensitive behavior.
