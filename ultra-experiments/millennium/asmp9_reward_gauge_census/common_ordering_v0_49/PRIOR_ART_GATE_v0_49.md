# ASMP-9 v0.49 prior-art gate

## Verdict

**Proceed only as a finite consolidation with one candidate-new elementary
minimality statement.**

The broad mathematics in v0.49 is classical. The result must not be presented
as a new theory of confidence ordering, multiobjective shortest paths, dynamic
programming, or discrete exact forms.

## Buehler confidence ordering

Robert Buehler's original construction makes the confidence limit minimal
subject to an imposed sample-space ordering:

- R. J. Buehler, "Confidence Intervals for the Product of Two Binomial
  Parameters," *Journal of the American Statistical Association* 52 (1957),
  482-493. DOI:
  [10.1080/01621459.1957.10501404](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1957.10501404).

Later work explicitly studies the dependence of finite and asymptotic
efficiency on the designated statistic or ordering:

- P. Kabaila, "Better Buehler confidence limits," *Statistics & Probability
  Letters* 52 (2001), 145-154. DOI:
  [10.1016/S0167-7152(00)00199-1](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0167-7152(00)00199-1).
- C. J. Lloyd and P. Kabaila, "On the Optimality and Limitations of Buehler
  Bounds," *Australian & New Zealand Journal of Statistics* 45 (2003),
  167-174. DOI:
  [10.1111/1467-842X.00272](https://doi.org/10.1111/1467-842X.00272).
- P. Kabaila and C. J. Lloyd, "The efficiency of Buehler confidence limits,"
  *Statistics & Probability Letters* 65 (2003), 21-28. DOI:
  [10.1016/S0167-7152(03)00215-3](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0167-7152(03)00215-3).

Therefore neither the dependence on ordering nor the fixed-order minimality
formula is a novelty claim.

## Dynamic programming and multiple objectives

The subset recurrence is Bellman's principle of optimality applied to the
Boolean-lattice DAG. Intersecting the tight-edge DAGs is a direct finite
shortest-path observation.

Multiple-objective shortest paths and their Pareto sets are established
operations-research subjects:

- J. C. N. Climaco and E. Q. V. Martins, "A bicriterion shortest path
  algorithm," *European Journal of Operational Research* 11 (1982),
  399-404. DOI:
  [10.1016/0377-2217(82)90205-3](https://doi.org/10.1016/0377-2217(82)90205-3).
- M. I. Henig, "The shortest path problem with two objective functions,"
  *European Journal of Operational Research* 25 (1986), 281-291. DOI:
  [10.1016/0377-2217(86)90092-5](https://doi.org/10.1016/0377-2217(86)90092-5).

The v0.49 common-tight-path theorem is consequently a lemma-level
specialization, not a new bicriterion shortest-path theorem.

## Exact one-forms and path independence

The zero-square-curl equivalence is the discrete Poincare/path-independence
principle on the contractible Boolean cube. Its ranking interpretation sits
inside established combinatorial Hodge theory:

- X. Jiang, L.-H. Lim, Y. Yao, and Y. Ye, "Statistical ranking and
  combinatorial Hodge theory," *Mathematical Programming* 127 (2011),
  203-244. DOI:
  [10.1007/s10107-010-0419-x](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10107-010-0419-x).

Calling the perturbation an "ordering gauge" is application-specific
terminology. It does not create a new exactness theorem.

## Candidate residual

The exact two-outcome/two-parameter Buehler experiment gives a
cardinality-minimal obstruction to one ordering being optimal for every
unrestricted finite decision risk. This search did not locate that precise
minimal statement in the Buehler literature.

The novelty status is:

```text
candidate-new elementary lemma; not established novelty.
```

Even if the witness is previously known, it remains useful as the sharp
negative control for the ASMP-9 access grammar.

## Durable contribution

The defensible contribution is the consolidation:

1. make the evidence ordering an explicit component of a
   decision-relative access certificate;
2. give an exact common-optimum certificate on the finite subset lattice;
3. isolate a sufficient quotient of set-bound objectives under which ordering
   choice is irrelevant; and
4. provide the smallest statistical witness showing why an unrestricted
   decision-independent ordering is impossible.

This closes one finite evidence-ordering subproblem. It does not resolve
continuous experiments, randomized confidence criteria, strategic
misspecification, or physical preference access.
