# ASMP-9 dynamic-response prior-art gate v0.66

## Verdict

The finite deterministic core is a classical state-identification problem for
sequential machines.  Adaptive distinguishing experiments, initial-state
versus terminal-state identification, incomplete distinguishing sequences,
and their synthesis algorithms all predate this project.  Version v0.66 may
record an ASMP-9 specialization with an explicit terminal-disturbance goal,
but it may not claim a new adaptive distinguishing-sequence theorem or
algorithm.

## Primary mathematical anchors

### Sequential-machine experiments

Edward F. Moore, "Gedanken-Experiments on Sequential Machines," in *Automata
Studies*, 1956, 129-153:

<https://doi.org/10.1515/9781400882618-006>

Moore introduced the experimental view of deterministic finite machines and
state distinguishability.

Arthur Gill, "State-Identification Experiments in Finite Automata,"
*Information and Control* 4 (1961), 132-154:

<https://doi.org/10.1016/S0019-9958(61)80003-X>

Gill explicitly classifies preset and adaptive experiments for identifying
initial and final states.  The v0.66 labelled-belief recursion sits inside
this established problem family.

### Generalized and algorithmic state identification

Hans-Dieter Burkhard, "Generalized Identification Experiments for Finite
Deterministic Automata," *Banach Center Publications* 7 (1982), 45-51:

<https://eudml.org/doc/209164>

David Lee and Mihalis Yannakakis, "Principles and Methods of Testing Finite
State Machines—A Survey," *Proceedings of the IEEE* 84(8), 1996, 1090-1123:

<https://doi.org/10.1109/5.533956>

These works place diagnostic, homing, preset, and adaptive experiments in a
mature automata-testing literature.

Robert M. Hierons and Uraz Cengiz Türker, "Incomplete Distinguishing Sequences
for Finite State Machines," *The Computer Journal* 58(11), 2015, 3089-3113:

<https://doi.org/10.1093/comjnl/bxv041>

Incomplete state identification and maximum distinguishable subsets are also
established objects, including hardness results.  The v0.66
maximum-identifiable-subset helper is therefore not novel.

## Empirical motivation, not theorem support

Eric J. Johnson, Gerald Häubl, and Anat Keinan, "Aspects of Endowment: A Query
Theory of Value Construction," *Journal of Experimental Psychology: Learning,
Memory, and Cognition* 33(3), 2007, 461-474:

<https://doi.org/10.1037/0278-7393.33.3.461>

Dan Simon, Daniel C. Krawczyk, Alison Bleicher, and Keith J. Holyoak, "The
Transience of Constructed Preferences," *Journal of Behavioral Decision
Making* 21 (2008), 1-14:

<https://doi.org/10.1002/bdm.575>

These studies motivate treating query-induced preference change as an
empirical possibility.  They do not validate the finite transducer used here,
and v0.66 does not reinterpret their experiments as an ASMP-9 test.

## Residual ASMP-9 contribution

The authorized contribution is an access ledger:

```text
static strategic report
  -> v0.65 outcome-menu/random-problem-selection boundary;

known deterministic response/update transducer
  -> classical adaptive initial-state identification;

terminal-distortion goal
  -> distinguish discovery from identify-then-alter and created consensus;

unknown, stochastic, strategic, or physically induced dynamics
  -> still open.
```

The exact three-way census and paired counterexamples make this distinction
executable inside the ASMP-9 program.  They are not claimed as new automata
mathematics.

## Claim boundary

Version v0.66 does not establish that humans or models have finite latent
value states, that their update law is known or deterministic, that terminal
restoration prevents transient manipulation, that the declared distortion has
moral meaning, or that a stable scalar value exists.  It does not resolve
ASMP-9.
