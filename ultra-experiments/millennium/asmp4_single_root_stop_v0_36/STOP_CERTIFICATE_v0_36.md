# ASMP-4 single-root stop certificate v0.36

## Decision

Stop autonomous ASMP-4 construction pending one of four concrete reopening
events below.  This is a harness-backed operational decision, not a
mathematical resolution and not an impossibility theorem for every formal
completion of the problem.

## 1. Sealed evidence

The central and independent harnesses seal six authoritative inputs:

1. the canonical ASMP-4 source;
2. the v0.6 sensor-registration fork;
3. the v0.8 randomness/disturbance-order fork;
4. the v0.33 nonfinite safe-closing region theorem;
5. the v0.34 compact public-connector theorem; and
6. the v0.35 robust public reset-atlas theorem.

The seals prevent a later source or claim change from silently inheriting this
decision.

## 2. What the canonical source fixes

Two structurally different parsers recover the same five completion
requirements.  They also recover six important architecture clauses:

- a registered causal code;
- a registered normally hyperbolic, locally controllable class;
- bounded uncertainty, delay, memory, control-authority, and disturbance
  conventions;
- separate plant, sensor, controller, and actuator components;
- no uncharged side channel; and
- universal safety over allowed disturbance sequences.

These clauses make the intended subject clear.  They do not define the domain
over which one region is to be proved.

## 3. Seven missing domain selectors

The canonical section supplies no formal selector for:

1. the admitted sensor grammar;
2. probability versus disturbance quantifier order;
3. a normally-hyperbolic predicate;
4. a local-controllability predicate;
5. public belief/reachable-set semantics;
6. safe-closing or reset-cell semantics; or
7. the admitted actuator-dictionary domain.

Calling these items “registered” tells a future formalization where choices
must be recorded, but does not make any particular choice normative.

## 4. The ambiguity changes the target

V0.6 holds the plant, evaluator, authority, channels, cost, and explicit source
obligations fixed.  Two source-compatible sensor registries give different
closed regions:

```text
computed sensor:   [1,infinity) x [1,infinity),
forced raw sensor: [2,infinity) x [1,infinity).
```

V0.8 independently shows that per-disturbance almost-sure safety and uniform
almost-sure safety differ on one continuous fixture, while the canonical
section contains no probability/disturbance ordering clause.  The missing
selectors are therefore material; they do not merely alter notation or a
proof technique.

## 5. The conditional mathematical route has been pursued

The successor chain addresses the strongest nonfinite alternative permitted
by the earlier stop audit:

- v0.33 proves the entire component region and global indexed union under
  sublinear safe closing, including coordinate transport and exact vector
  finite-horizon corrections;
- v0.34 derives safe closing from compact connected public all-pairs connector
  certificates; and
- v0.35 constructs fixed-reset safe closing from robust Lipschitz tubes, strict
  margins, charged source labels, and charged actuator words.

Each of the five canonical requirements now has explicit conditional evidence.
None has a frozen-scope proof because all five still quantify over an
unselected state/code/uncertainty domain.  An additional local fixture can
strengthen one chosen completion, but cannot choose that completion on behalf
of the source.

## 6. Single residual root

After v0.35, the dependency audit has one unresolved upstream root:

```text
formal normative registry or attributable semantic adjudication.
```

The nonfinite variational formula, compact public connector bridge, robust
reset-atlas bridge, finite-margin law, and material boundary witnesses are all
present under checkable hypotheses.  A theorem that “the canonical class
implies those hypotheses” cannot be stated until the canonical class is a
predicate.  Likewise, a larger finite census cannot decide whether raw or
computed sensing, or which randomness quantifier order, was intended.

## 7. Exhaustive stopping logic

The decision predicate has five Boolean inputs:

```text
source ambiguous,
material fork present,
conditional theorem route closed to its hypotheses,
external adjudication present,
sealed premise error present.
```

Stop exactly when the first three are true and the last two are false.  The
central and independent implementations enumerate all 32 assignments.  There
is exactly one stop row, and it is the current row.  Flipping any one current
premise to its reopening value clears the stop.

## Reopening conditions

Resume work only if at least one of the following occurs:

1. A formal normative registry specifies the plant class, sensor grammar,
   randomness/disturbance order, public state, cost conventions, and actuator
   domain.
2. Attributable external adjudication selects a formal interpretation of the
   frozen wording.
3. A demonstrated error invalidates a sealed fork, theorem, parser, or
   dependency premise.
4. A registry-independent equivalence theorem proves that the material forks
   collapse for the intended completion.

Condition 1 may reopen substantial mathematics: a newly fixed registry might
or might not satisfy v0.33–v0.35.  Condition 2 supplies the missing semantic
authority.  Conditions 3 and 4 directly undermine current stop premises.

## Conclusion

Continuing to add autonomous fixtures would produce more correct conditional
results while leaving the requested canonical target unchanged.  The honest
endpoint is therefore to preserve the verified theorem chain, report ASMP-4 as
unresolved, and wait for evidence that changes the single residual root.
