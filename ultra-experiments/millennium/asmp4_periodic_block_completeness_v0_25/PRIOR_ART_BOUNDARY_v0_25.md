# Prior-art boundary v0.25

The finite-graph ingredients of v0.25 are classical and are not claimed as new.

- Richard M. Karp, *A Characterization of the Minimum Cycle Mean in a
  Digraph*, Discrete Mathematics 23 (1978), 309-311. The archived
  [Berkeley technical report](https://www2.eecs.berkeley.edu/Pubs/TechRpts/1977/29121.html)
  gives the minimum-cycle-mean characterization and algorithm used by each
  scalarized component support value.
- Krystyna Ziemian, *Rotation Sets for Subshifts of Finite Type*, Fundamenta
  Mathematicae 146 (1995), 189-201. The
  [journal record and paper](https://www.impan.pl/en/publishing-house/journals-and-series/fundamenta-mathematicae/all/146/2/108781/rotation-sets-for-subshifts-of-finite-type)
  prove that a transitive finite-type shift with a locally constant vector
  potential has a convex polyhedral rotation set and periodic rotation vectors
  dense in it.

A strongly connected finite scheduler graph is the relevant edge-shift case,
and its read/write edge-cost vector is a locally constant two-dimensional
potential. The cycle-polytope and periodic-density conclusions should therefore
be read as a self-contained specialization and executable verification of
classical structure.

The repository-specific contribution is narrower:

1. map the two vector coordinates to separately charged realized read/write
   transcript growth in the frozen ASMP-4 architecture;
2. connect the component support values to v0.24's weighted variational family;
3. expose the irreversible-SCC fork where minimizing across components
   convexifies the existential capacity region and adds the false point
   `(2,2)`; and
4. preserve the distinction between a finite public scheduler abstraction and
   a proof that arbitrary nonlinear control admits such an abstraction.

No literature-level novelty claim should be made for minimum mean cycles,
cycle decomposition, rotation polytopes, or periodic approximation themselves.
