## General-game background

Nonmonotonicity of Nash-equilibrium sets under changes to the available
strategy sets is not a new game-theoretic phenomenon. It follows immediately
from the equilibrium definition: adding a strategy adds possible unilateral
deviations and can invalidate a previous best-response profile.

The literature on sustainable equilibria makes strategy addition and deletion
explicit. Govindan, Laraki, and Pahl,
[“On sustainable equilibria”](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jet.2023.105736),
studies when equilibria survive or can be made unique in larger games formed by
adding strategies. This is adjacent background, not a claim that its object is
identical to bounded source-reading program catalogs.

Combined with Tennenholtz's unrestricted program-equilibrium folk theorem and
the simulation-based characterizations already cited in v0.1, the conservative
position is:

- general equilibrium-set nonmonotonicity is classical;
- literal source-code brittleness is a known criticism of early program
  equilibrium constructions;
- the v0.1 contribution is the complete finite census under a declared budget,
  the exact source-syntax witness, and the syntax/extensional decomposition of
  cooperative deaths; and
- the finding that 134 extensional catalogs still lose cooperation shows that
  the census is not exhausted by the literal-code critique, but it is still a
  finite instrument result rather than a new general theorem.
