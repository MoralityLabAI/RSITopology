# When More Programs Destroy Cooperation

## A small exact experiment found a sharp boundary between harmless choice and destabilizing temptation

More options are usually treated as a benefit. Give an agent more strategies,
more expressive code, or a larger space of possible commitments, and it should
be able to preserve everything it could do before while gaining new
possibilities.

That intuition fails for equilibrium.

When the available strategy set grows, the old cooperative strategy remains
available. But so do new deviations. A cooperative outcome that was stable in
the smaller game can disappear in the larger one because one of the newly
admitted programs makes betrayal profitable.

We built a deliberately small experiment to locate that failure exactly. The
result is not a claim about language models or deployed AI systems. It is a
complete census of a finite source-reading program game, designed to expose
the mechanism without hiding behind sampling noise or an optimizer.

The boundary was sharp:

| Temptation payoff relative to cooperation reward | Catalogs where budget growth destroyed cooperation |
|---|---:|
| `T <= R` | **0 of 512** |
| `T > R` | **174 of 512** |

At the boundary itself, a deviation tied the cooperative reward and did not
destroy equilibrium under the registered rule that only strictly profitable
deviations count. Move temptation one hundredth above the reward, and the
canonical witness fails.

## The game

Each player chooses a deterministic program. The program can read the public
source identifier of its opponent and then cooperate or defect. We enumerated
all 512 ordered catalogs generated by the registered three-program language.
We then increased the admissible program budget and checked every pure
equilibrium under two frozen payoff families and exact rational arithmetic.

The important word is *admissible*. These programs are not proof systems, and
they are not yet doing verification in the stronger sense used in reflective
agent research. They are source-conditioned lookup strategies. The experiment
asks what happens when a game admits more such strategies.

The answer depends on the temptation payoff `T` and the reward for mutual
cooperation `R`, fixed here at `R=3`.

Suppose an existing program pair produces mutual cooperation. Each player
receives `R`. A newly admitted unilateral deviation can produce one of the
registered outcome payoffs: reward `R`, sucker payoff `S`, punishment `P`, or
temptation `T`. In both payoff families, `S` and `P` are no greater than `R`.
Therefore, while `T <= R`, no new deviation can improve strictly on mutual
cooperation.

Once `T > R`, that arithmetic protection disappears. A new program can exploit
an opponent that continues to cooperate. The complete census tells us how
often the expanded catalog actually contains such a destabilizing deviation:
174 times out of 512.

The knife edge appears directly in the canonical witness:

| `T` | Cooperative margin | Survives? |
|---:|---:|---|
| `2.99` | `+0.01` | yes |
| `3.00` | `0` | yes, under no-strict-improvement semantics |
| `3.01` | `-0.01` | no |

The elementary proof explains why the lower region is empty. The census shows
the size and internal structure of the vulnerable region above it.

## Was this merely brittle source-code matching?

A familiar objection to source-reading agents is that literal program identity
is a poor stand-in for behavior. A strategy might cooperate with one source
label and defect against another even when the two programs behave identically.
That creates a cheap kind of brittleness.

We separated that mechanism from the stronger one.

An **extensional catalog** in this experiment is one satisfying the registered
restriction that behaviorally identical programs are treated identically even
when their public source labels differ. The 174 cooperative deaths partitioned
exactly as follows:

| Mechanism | Catalogs |
|---|---:|
| Extensional catalogs admitting a genuinely new behavioral deviation | 134 |
| Non-extensional syntax-equivalence exploits | 16 |
| Other non-extensional deaths | 24 |
| **Total** | **174** |

So 40 deaths occurred among the 138 non-extensional catalogs, including the 16
clean syntax-label exploits. But 134 occurred among the 374 catalogs that
passed the extensionality restriction.

Quotienting by behavior removes one real failure mode. It does not restore
monotonicity. A genuinely different new strategy can still destroy a
program-manufactured cooperative equilibrium.

## Manufactured cooperation is the fragile kind

The control comparison helps explain the result.

In a Stag Hunt, mutual cooperation is already a Nash equilibrium of the base
game. Adding source-conditioned programs does not create the underlying reason
to cooperate, so enlarging the catalog does not readily take it away.

In Prisoner's Dilemma- and Chicken-ordered games, the cooperative equilibria in
this experiment are manufactured by conditional programs. They survive because
each program responds to the other in a way that makes deviation unattractive.
Catalog growth can introduce a response that breaks that construction.

The experiment is therefore measuring the fragility of
**program-manufactured cooperation**, with base-game-Nash cooperation serving
as the stable control. It is not claiming that every form of cooperation
declines when agents become more expressive.

## How much of the result is robust?

We evaluated 32,064 cooperative-equilibrium margins for profiles with at least
one nontrivial admitted deviation.

- 12,384 margins were strictly positive and received the registered robustness
  certificate.
- 19,680 were exactly zero.
- No checked profile occupied an unreported intermediate category.

For a positive margin `m`, perturbing every payoff entry by at most `m/4`
leaves at least `m/2` of worst-case residual margin. A zero-margin profile has
no such protection and can change status under an arbitrarily small adverse
perturbation.

The point is not that every equilibrium in the census is robust. It is that
the experiment records precisely which ones are and does not turn ties into
evidence.

## What this result does not say

The exact `T=R` boundary concerns the destruction of existing cooperative
**pure** equilibria. It does not describe the entire equilibrium payoff
correspondence, which changes in additional lower-temptation regions. Nor does
the experiment characterize mixed equilibria, unrestricted program languages,
bounded proof search, negotiation dynamics, language-model cooperation, or
deployed systems.

It also does not yet define an ordinary persistence module. The admitted
program sets and their profitable-deviation graphs grow with budget, but the
equilibrium sets derived from those graphs can be born or die and are not
nested. A future zigzag or constructible-persistence treatment may be useful;
it has not been supplied here.

## Why this small result is useful

The main contribution is an instrument and a boundary, not a sweeping theorem.
The experiment turns a broad question—whether stronger source-conditioned
agents cooperate more reliably—into an exact finite object with visible
failure mechanisms.

It gives a sharper behavioral hypothesis for later language-model work:

- a flat cooperation curve would suggest that agents are not conditioning
  meaningfully on source information;
- a simple threshold could indicate coarse source conditioning; and
- cooperation that appears and then dies as the admissible strategy or
  verification budget grows would be a distinctive signature of strategic
  source conditioning.

That hypothesis remains untested in language models. The present result earns
the right to test it without pretending that the finite census already answers
the empirical question.

There is also a broader lesson for control and verification. Increasing the
expressive power available to an agent does not merely add capabilities. It
changes the deviation set against which every earlier agreement must remain
stable. More options preserve feasible actions, but they need not preserve
equilibria.

That is the paradox in one sentence:

> The cooperative program is still available. Cooperation is gone because a
> better deviation has become available too.

## Reproducibility

The result comes from a prospectively registered exact CPU census using
rational arithmetic. Seven scientific gates passed. Four v0.2 implementation
tests and four earlier regression tests passed, and an independent
implementation reproduced the primary death counts.
