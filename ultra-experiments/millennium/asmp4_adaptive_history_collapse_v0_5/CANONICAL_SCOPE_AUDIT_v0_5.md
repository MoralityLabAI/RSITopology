**Superseded scope decision.** The v0.6 registration-fork theorem resolves the
first interpretation question below internally. A forced raw sensor and a
computed sensor have exact corners `(2,1)` and `(1,1)` on the same plant under

## Question

Do the v0.2/v0.3 bilateral relay theorems prove the literal canonical ASMP-4
statement, or only a narrower finite prefix fixture?

## Normal-form dependency audit

The structural collapse is not inferred from the v0.4/v0.5 census. It follows
from two computation-mobility facts in the canonical serial chain.

| Normal form | Required fact | Where it is used | Failure boundary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Upstream | The controller output is a deterministic causal function only of the read history and public architecture state. The sensor generated that history and may perform the same computation by the emission deadline. | v0.2 Theorem 1; v0.3 Theorem 1 | Controller-local plant information, private randomness/delivery state, computational restrictions, or a deadline-incompatible schedule |
| Downstream | The actuator may retain memory and compute the original controller from a relayed read history before applying the original decoder. | v0.3 Theorem 2 | Restricted decoder syntax/memory, actuator-local information that changes the registered code, or incompatible timing |
| Bilateral metric copy | Both ports admit the copied tree and charge the same relabel-invariant, deterministic-prefix-invariant functional. | v0.3 Theorem 3 | Heterogeneous objectives, peak alphabets, syntax restrictions, noise/reliability coordinates, or port-specific costs |

Under those premises, every safe code can be replaced by a behaviorally
identical code whose two transcript trees are copies of either original port.
The equality of coordinate infima and the diagonal-quadrant region then follow
without a plant-specific assumption. The finite harnesses are regression and
boundary evidence, not the proof of this implication.

## Quantifiers not supplied by the finite successor

The expected-read/worst-write models in v0.4/v0.5 are deliberately different
from the canonical worst-case complete-transcript metric. Their adaptive
rectangles strengthen the boundary diagnosis but cannot be used as proof of
the v0.1 scalar variational theorem.

Likewise, the current repository does not prove probability-of-safety results,
noisy-channel reliability or anytime-capacity theorems, arbitrary private or
non-FIFO delivery processes, computationally bounded component results, or a
closed-form entropy for every normally hyperbolic nonlinear plant. Those
objects either change the registered coordinates or require new mathematics.

## Audit decision

The deterministic, noiseless, no-side-channel serial class closed under both
computation moves and equipped with the canonical shared transcript-cardinality
cost is covered by the bilateral normal-form proof. This audit originally
treated that closure as the literal registration and retained **candidate
negative resolution with a positive replacement characterization**. The v0.6
fork shows that a coherent forced-raw registration lacks upstream closure, so
the current status is instead conditional structural classification.

It must not claim externally accepted resolution of every broader reading.
Two issues remain outside local harness authority:

1. whether the canonical phrase “registered causal code” intended all and only
   the normal-form-closed timing/computation conventions above; and
2. whether the operational safe-policy infimum is accepted as the requested
   variational characterization for the full nonlinear class.

The decisive next evidence for those issues is an external proof/scope review
or a counterexample inside the written normal-form premises. Enlarging the
finite prefix-code census cannot decide them.
