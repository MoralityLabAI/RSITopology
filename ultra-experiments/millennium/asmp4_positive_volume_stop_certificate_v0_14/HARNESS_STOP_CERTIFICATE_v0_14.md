## Decision

Stop local construction and request normative registration of the sensor observation/computation class.

This decision is narrower than a mathematical resolution of ASMP-4. It says the current bounded-harness program has reached a logical boundary: another plant, longer horizon, or denser enumeration cannot decide which compatible sensor registry the frozen source intended.

## Evidence that now survives

The v0.13 plant has bounded authority `[-2,10]`, arbitrary registered mode disturbances, and safe set `S^1 x [-1,1] x {-3,-1,1,3}` of normalized volume 2. Its feedback-cancelled invariant circle is compact, connected, one-dimensional, normally expanded, and locally controllable.

For this same plant, safe set, evaluator, disturbance convention, timing, and actuator authority:

- the computed sensor completion has exact region `[2,infinity) x [2,infinity)`;
- the forced raw completion has exact region `[3,infinity) x [2,infinity)`.

The difference is not a finite-horizon artifact. At every horizon `T`, normal expansion contributes `2^T` necessary words. The two control-relevant mode fibers contribute `2^T`, while the raw registry is forced to distinguish `4^T` mode words. Thus computed reads and writes have `4^T` words, and raw reads have `8^T`. For every fixed positive initial radius, the exact normal correction is `ceil(rho*2^T)`.

The complete predecessor chain passes 174 tests before this certificate. ## Why the remaining step is normative

The canonical text defines rates for a “registered causal code,” separates plant, sensor, controller, and actuator, and acknowledges architecture-dependent tradeoffs. It does not define the sensor observation map, the computation closure inside the sensor, raw-mode injectivity, or a coarsest-sufficient-statistic convention.

Five counterfactual clauses were checked. Permitting the `q`-fiber quotient or charging a coarsest sufficient statistic selects the computed region. Requiring raw-mode injectivity or a calibrated four-label sensor selects the raw region. Making the registry a theorem parameter yields a family instead of a source-selected single region. All make the target determinate; none is present in the source.

A finite harness can falsify a proposed formal completion, but it cannot manufacture a normative clause in the frozen problem statement. Continuing to enumerate more instances would therefore produce further conditional theorems without resolving the selection question.

## Four reopening conditions

Resume this line of work only if at least one of the following occurs:

1. A normative sensor observation/computation registry is added.
2. An attributable proof or implementation error invalidates the positive-volume fork.
3. A specified perturbation/noise model invalidates or collapses both exact completions.
4. An attributable theorem or external review maps the existing wording to one unique formal class.

Absent one of these events, the evidence supports stopping bounded local construction. The appropriate next action is external review or a canonical erratum, not another fixture.
